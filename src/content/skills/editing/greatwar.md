As someone with absolutely no musical talent I've always watched scoring featurettes for film and games with awe — so getting the chance to work on one was an exciting challenge. It was a large undertaking given the sheer amount of footage to catalogue, and later synchronise.

I insisted on one thing — that the footage of the players was properly synchronised to the final score.

When I seek out the behind the scenes for a project it's because I want to get some version of the experience of being in the room while it was made. Seeing the energy of a thing you love brought to life. So when, in many featurettes, they use the general footage of the musicians as pure B-Roll over the general score I feel it never achieves that feeling. Not being there for the shooting meant that I needed to synchronise each shot to the score, sometimes relying on checking in-shot sheet music to confirm timings.

While those moments may not be the <i>exact</i> stake from the musicians this was a feat which I'm proud to say was achieved; and well worth having this stuck in my head for months after.
